## Hole in the theory (Philip): interactions with the slit

Philip expects interactions with the slit material (Coulomb, even without collisions), for example rebounds off the
slit edges pushing particles across the axis. In the model the wall is psi = 0: a rigid external boundary, not
matter. No-crossing of the axis follows from the mirror symmetry plus one-body guidance (a single-valued v in 3D).
With the wall's charges as dynamic bodies, guidance lives on the joint (particle, wall) configuration. The 3D paths
are then projections and may cross. So no-crossing is an artifact of the external-potential approximation.
Measured analogues: vdW / Casimir-Polder at slit edges in atom and molecule diffraction (Grisenti 1999, Arndt
C70), and image charges for electrons. QM treats these as a static V, which still leaves out the wall's response.
Links to which-slit (#3) and back-reaction (#4). Test: does a wall of interacting bodies give axis crossings, and do
the fringes and Born statistics survive?

Philip: "we think that the particles and the slits are independent, they absolutely are not." The model assumes
independence three ways: the wall is a fixed psi = 0 boundary with no state, the particle does not feed the wave, and
the wall never feels the particle. A non-independent version: wall cells as bodies on the same lattice with their own
state, coupled to the particle by the same face rule, with back-reaction. That merges next #3 (which-slit) and #4
(back-reaction). The idealised run stays as the reference.

Philip: a field is not generated by a particle. It is always there and the particle disturbs it. Model consequence:
a background state on the whole lattice (the slit run's lattice is empty outside the packet), particles and slits
as deformations of it, conserved redistribution with no source terms, and interaction as overlapping disturbances.
The vortex is an analogue only, since the shape of the disturbance is unknown and may be imposed as a coarse rule.

Philip: the lattice is never empty and has no holes; like absolute zero, there is no zero state. There is always a
base layer, the discrete layer, like an elastic band that cannot break and has a finite stretch. This is for DST:
there, slits and particles are deformations of the band (stretched or stiff, never missing), with the finite stretch
as the cutoff. The Bohmian slit is its own model (a reference for accepted results), so its psi = 0 wall and empty
lattice are legitimate there (Philip).

## hydrogen_swarm/ (README there)

Hydrogen 1s on the Kelvin lattice: Copenhagen (lattice ground state) next to an electron made of N constituents with
a von Weizsacker self-pressure (no Coulomb between constituents), relaxed to a static equilibrium around a fixed
proton, then leaning in a field F = 0.01 au. At a0 = 4 cells: Copenhagen E -0.5015, alpha 4.468 (exact -0.5, 4.5).
Swarm (640k constituents): E -0.5086, alpha 4.10 (symmetric +-F), and a density change matching Copenhagen's. The
remaining gaps are finite N (floor bias ~ 1/sqrt N) and convergence.

Side discussions: fine structure is irrelevant for 1s (alpha^2 ~ 5e-5) and matters at n = 2. Real alpha can be
handled perturbatively (Breit-Pauli plus the measured Lamb shift, no renormalisation); the Dirac walk with an
exaggerated alpha is for spin-orbit from the rule. QED limits: tested to uranium, supercritical Z ~ 170 unobserved.
plamn's view3d (mathplot) can take Kelvin points as is; worth it for n = 2 / n = 4 shapes and live runs.

## hydrogen_pair/ (README there)

Proton as a population. References on the lattice: exact two-body (relative coordinate, reduced mass) vs mean-field
(two clouds, each in the other's average potential). Mean-field misses even at M = 1836: E -0.4536 vs -0.4997
(+9.5%), alpha 15.2 vs 4.51. The proton pays a zero-point energy in the electron cloud's well and slides inside it.
At M = 10: +44%, alpha 22.6 vs 5.99. So independent populations cannot be hydrogen; correlation (the electron follows
the proton) is essential. Unlinked swarms would converge to mean-field (the self-pressure is exact for one body).
