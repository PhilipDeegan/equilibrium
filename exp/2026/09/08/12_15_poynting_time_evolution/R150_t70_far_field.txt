R=150 octagon Poynting-ring far-field / boundary assessment
=============================================================
Checkpoint chain: fine_R150_octagon_t{10,20,30,40,50,60,70}_state*.h5
(fresh IC at t=0, --hops 8 --mode octagon --threads 6, restart-chained in
10-time-unit legs; see fine_R150_octagon_t*.log for exact per-leg commands)

1. Far-field radial |phi| profile check (t=50)
-----------------------------------------------
Script: probe_farfield_radial_profile.py
Run:
  python3 probe_farfield_radial_profile.py \
    --state fine_R150_octagon_t50_state_step0009750.h5 \
    --r-min 30 --r-max 130 --n-bins 30
Output: fine_R150_octagon_t50_state_step0009750_farfield_profile.{npz,png}

Result: phi_rms(r) is NOT a single power law over r=[30,130].
  - r=30-65:   clean decay, fitted slope ~ -2.67 (close to dipole-like 1/r^3)
  - r=70-126:  flattens to a plateau, fitted slope ~ -0.32, amplitude ~1.0-1.3e-6
  - sponge starts at r_inner ~ 127.5 (= 0.85 * r_max_present=150.0)

Interpretation at the time: the flat tail seen at R=100 (r=30-85, amplitude
~5-20e-6) resolves into real decay with more domain room, but the plateau
itself persists (just pushed further out, r~70 instead of r~30) at a similar
absolute amplitude regardless of domain size -- read as a background noise
floor, not genuine propagating far-field content, since a real 1/r^n falloff
would keep decreasing rather than plateau.

CAVEAT ADDED BELOW: this profile was taken at t=50, i.e. BEFORE the sponge
reflection event described in section 2 (predicted arrival ~t=68) reaches
the near-ring region. It should NOT be contaminated by that specific
reflection. However, the sponge's imperfect absorption (section 2) means
earlier/different reflections at other frequencies or from other emission
times could in principle already be present in the r=70-126 plateau at t=50
-- the "noise floor" interpretation should be treated as provisional pending
a real absorption-quality check (see section 3).

2. Sponge reflection event, t60-t70 leg
-----------------------------------------
Symptom: S[r=20] (energy-flux magnitude, equatorial shell average) rose from
~1.0e-08 (t=60) to a peak of 6.1e-08 (t=67.44), then declined to 2.9e-08 by
t=70 -- a ~6x excursion, well above the smooth monotonic decay seen in every
prior leg (t10 through t60: S20 peak values 1.9e-5, 4.4e-7, 7.6e-8, 4.6e-8,
2.1e-8, 1.6e-8 -- steadily decreasing until this leg).

All five measured shells (r=8,12,16,20,24) rose and fell IN LOCKSTEP, peaking
within a narrow window (t=67.4-68.2), not with a radius-dependent time lag
(which would indicate a wavefront newly sweeping outward through the shells).
Amplitude was overwhelmingly concentrated at r=20 (peak 6.1e-08) vs r=8 (peak
2.3e-09, ~26x smaller) -- r=20 sits almost exactly at the ring radius
(ring_r=19.79 for this IC).

Rendered XZ cross-sections at t=62.5/65.5/68.0/70.0 (fine_R150_octagon_t70_
frames/) show no obviously new spatial feature -- same qualitative "cross"
pattern and similar log10|S| dynamic range at all four times. Consistent
with the effect being concentrated in the equatorial sector used for the
shell measurement (near the ring's own plane) rather than the polar-axis
region the XZ slab mostly shows, and with the color scale being per-frame
normalized (a moderate absolute change is not obviously visible by eye).

Working hypothesis: partial reflection off the sponge boundary of the
strong initial burst launched when the toroidal winding is seeded at t=0
(t10 leg shows by far the largest transient: S20 peak 1.9e-5 during t=0-10).

Round-trip light-crossing check:
  ring radius            r_ring    = 19.79
  sponge inner radius    r_inner   = 127.5   (0.85 * 150.0, from radial
                                               profile check r_max_present)
  substrate wave speed   c         = sqrt(10) ~= 3.162   (per casimir.py
                                               docstring, axis-aligned)
  one-way distance                 = 127.5 - 19.79 = 107.71
  round-trip distance              = 215.42
  predicted round-trip time        = 215.42 / 3.162 ~= 68.1

Observed peak: t ~= 67.4-68.2. Predicted: t ~= 68.1. Match to within ~1%.

Conclusion: the sponge (--sponge 0.15, --damp-max 0.003, quadratic_sponge
BC, r_inner ~ 0.85 * r_max) is NOT fully absorbing -- a real, non-negligible
fraction of the t=0 burst is reflecting back into the domain rather than
being damped, and it is large enough to dominate the r=20 shell signal by
~6x over its pre-reflection baseline.

3. Implication for far-field / noise-floor conclusions
---------------------------------------------------------
- The t=50 radial profile (section 1) predates this reflection's arrival at
  the near-ring shells (t~68), so it should be reasonably clean of THIS
  specific echo. But the sponge quality problem is generic, not a one-off:
  any future profile/trace measurement taken after ~t=68 (this checkpoint
  chain) will have reflection contamination at least in the near-ring
  shells, and possibly further out too depending on where the reflected
  wavefront currently sits.
- The "flat tail = noise floor, not real 1/r decay" conclusion from section
  1 should be treated as provisional until sponge absorption is actually
  verified quantitatively (e.g. via probe_sponge_quality_v3.py, or by
  comparing runs with tightened sponge parameters).

5. Tightened-sponge re-run (2026-09-10) -- damp_max=0.01 made the reflection
   WORSE, not better
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Followed up on step 4's first suggestion. Before spending R150 compute, tuned
damp_max cheaply at small R using probe_sponge_quality_v3.py (R=30, script
defaults --mode square --hops 4, sponge_fraction=0.15 held fixed to match
production, damp_max swept 0.003/0.005/0.007/0.01/0.015/0.02). Result: a clean
non-monotonic optimum at damp_max=0.01 -- outer-shell late-time |S| dropped
~5-8x vs damp_max=0.003 (r=20: 1.157e-8 -> 2.32e-9; r=24: 9.73e-9 -> 1.26e-9),
degrading again above 0.01 (classic over-damping/impedance-mismatch signature).
Widening sponge_fraction to 0.35 helped further but was rejected -- at R=150
that would cost 30 units of exactly the far-field domain room this whole R150
push was for (r_inner 127.5 -> 97.5), so frac was kept at 0.15 with only
damp_max raised.

A direct raw-lattice-field diff (new script: probe_sponge_direct_field_diff.py,
same IC run twice with damp_max=0.003 vs 0.01, shared non-percentile color
scale, R=30/t=10, well past one-way sponge arrival but before round-trip)
confirmed the mechanism was real and geometrically localized: interior field
identical to noise level (mean|S| 4.637e-7 vs 4.639e-7, r<0.5*r_inner), with a
genuine ~13% reduction confined to the sponge edge itself (r>=r_inner: 4.950e-8
vs 4.292e-8). This also explained an earlier user observation that individual
R150 t=45 frames "looked very different" (old vmin=-13.7 vs new vmin=-16.3 in
log10|S|, vmax nearly identical) -- a real but edge-localized effect getting
visually amplified by each frame's independent percentile-based color
normalization (matplotlib figure titles print the actual vmin/vmax per frame;
worth checking those numbers directly before trusting a color-only comparison
across separately-rendered/normalized frames or movies).

Ran the full R150 octagon damp_max=0.01 chain anyway (7 legs, t0->t70,
--threads 8, same --sponge 0.15, out-prefix tightsponge_R150_octagon_t*) to
check the actual t~68 reflection bump. Direct shell-trace comparison against
the original damp_max=0.003 chain (fine_R150_octagon_t70.log):

  t       S[r=20] old(0.003)   S[r=20] new(0.01)
  60.0    1.01e-8              1.08e-8   (baseline matches, pre-reflection)
  65.5    4.66e-8              1.00e-7
  66.0    5.35e-8              1.02e-7   <- new peak (EARLIER than old's)
  67.5    6.05e-8   <- old peak  7.46e-8
  70.0    2.93e-8              5.40e-8   (rising again, not settled)

The tightened sponge made the reflection WORSE: peak ~1.7x higher (1.02e-7 vs
6.05e-8), arrives ~1.5 time units earlier (t~66 vs t~67.5), and hasn't cleanly
decayed by t=70 (new shows a renewed uptick at the end where old was still
monotonically falling) -- opposite of the small-R30 sweep's prediction.

Root cause (identified, not yet fixed): the R=30 damp_max sweep used
probe_sponge_quality_v3.py's script DEFAULTS, --mode square --hops 4 -- never
overridden to match the actual production geometry, --mode octagon --hops 8.
Different ring mode/hops means different emitted frequency content, and a
sponge/PML-style absorbing layer is inherently frequency-sensitive -- a
damp_max that's well-matched (low reflection coefficient) for one wavelength
can be badly mismatched (high reflection coefficient, even reflection-
amplifying) for another. The sweep tuned against the wrong ring geometry
entirely, so its "optimal" damp_max=0.01 doesn't transfer to production.
