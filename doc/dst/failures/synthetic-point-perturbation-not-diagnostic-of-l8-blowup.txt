CONTEXT: Using synthetic point/small-radius perturbation test cases (Parts
32-35 of the old L8-blowup investigation log) as a cheap proxy for
diagnosing the real production-spheromak L8 base-update blowup
(H/H0=134).

WHAT FAILED: The synthetic point/small-radius perturbation tests showed
oscillatory, eta_h-responsive behavior driven by high-frequency content.
This looked like useful diagnostic signal at the time, but it does not
match the real blowup's behavior.

ROOT CAUSE: The real production spheromak IC is smooth and does not
carry the high-frequency content that the synthetic point perturbations
were probing — the two are testing genuinely different regimes. The
real blowup's actual driver turned out to be under-resolution
(cells_across too small relative to the spheromak's own diameter at L8),
not high-frequency/eta_h-sensitive dynamics.

LESSON: Don't reuse the Parts 32-35 synthetic point-perturbation results
to reason about the real production spheromak IC's blowup — they are
not diagnostic of it and answer a different question (high-frequency
sensitivity) than the one that mattered (resolution relative to object
size). When validating an AMR blowup fix, test on an IC with the same
smoothness/scale characteristics as the real target, not a convenient
synthetic stand-in.
