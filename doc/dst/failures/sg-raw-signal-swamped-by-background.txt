CONTEXT: stern_gerlach.py's original 2026-09-01 test compared raw circ_z_com(+1) vs circ_z_com(-1) at a fixed late step (30000) to look for chirality-dependent magnetic deflection, and found ratio≈+1.01 (not ≈-1), reported as a clean null.

WHAT FAILED: That null was later shown to be a diagnostic-design artifact, not evidence against chirality coupling. The "SG magnet" injects a raw azimuthal phi pattern additively into the SAME array as the particle's own spin, and since step() dynamics are linear, the measured circ_z_com on the combined field equals circ_z_com(background alone) + [spin-dependent piece] — and the injected background term is large and spin-independent, dominating the raw comparison.

ROOT CAUSE: Manually isolating the background (circ_z_com(spin) minus circ_z_com(bg-alone)) revealed a near-exactly antisymmetric response (ratio 0.99-1.00) while the winding's circulation was still intact — the opposite of the raw-comparison conclusion. The original null was reading raw, background-dominated values at a fixed step (30000) where tot_circ had already dropped below 1% of its initial value, by which point whatever real signal existed had also mostly decayed away underneath the swamping background term.

LESSON: When a probe additively injects a large background field into the same array being measured, and the dynamics are linear, always isolate the signal-of-interest (subtract a background-alone control) before concluding a null — a raw combined-field comparison can hide a real, opposite-sign effect underneath a dominant spin-independent term. Also evaluate at a point where the quantity of interest (here tot_circ) is still substantially intact, not an arbitrarily fixed late step where it may have already radiated away.
