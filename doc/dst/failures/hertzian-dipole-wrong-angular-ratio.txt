CONTEXT: Tested whether the Poynting-ring probes' E/B convention
(E:=-dphi/dt, B:=curl(phi)) reproduces the classical Hertzian-dipole
radiation pattern (S ~ sin^2(theta), so equator flux should greatly
exceed pole flux) for both a continuous-sampled source and a
genuinely lattice-native discrete two-cell dipole.

WHAT FAILED: equator/pole ratio pinned at ~0.3 for the continuous
source (swept source radius 1.5-6.0 cells, 15 to 1211 source cells,
no change) and ~0.31 for the lattice-native two-cell dipole — wrong
direction entirely (should be >>1, got <1) and, for the lattice-native
case, a suspiciously tiny absolute magnitude (~1e-18).

ROOT CAUSE: not resolved, but three plausible explanations were
explicitly tested and ruled out:
1. epsilon0/mu0 convention choice — any self-consistent split only
   rescales S by a positive constant; can't flip a ratio or explain
   18 orders of magnitude.
2. source under-resolution — ratio stayed pinned at ~0.3 across a
   15x to 1211-cell range of source sizes.
3. source discreteness — the exact lattice-native two-cell dipole
   gave the same wrong ratio as the continuous sphere.
The real cause is presumed to be the E/B convention itself
(E:=-dphi/dt, B:=curl(phi)), consistent with its independent failure
on the local Poynting-theorem check (see
poynting-local-theorem-check-fails.txt) — same convention, same
failure mode, different test.

LESSON: don't re-attempt Hertzian-dipole validation under this
convention, and don't re-check source resolution, source discreteness,
or epsilon0/mu0 splitting as possible causes — all three were already
ruled out. Any future Hertzian-dipole check needs a derived-from-
first-principles S/flux identity first (see
incompatible-em-field-conventions.txt), not another guessed
convention.
