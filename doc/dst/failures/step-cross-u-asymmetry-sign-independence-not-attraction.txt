CONTEXT: electron_positron_collision.py, extended with real energy-weighted centroid tracking (dz_up/dz_dn), used to test whether the step_cross-generated near/far u-asymmetry between paired objects is a genuine attraction/repulsion (electrostatic-like or gravity-like) mechanism. Run under clean gate="opposed", ref=50 (R=60, scale=8, z-sep=20, n-steps=3000) to avoid the barrier/m0=2.49 contamination (see companion failure file).

WHAT FAILED: both an electron/positron pair and an electron/electron (--same-sign) pair drifted toward the midplane in the SAME direction, with the same-sign pair showing ~4x larger magnitude drift, not an opposite sign. A genuine electrostatic-like (or gravity-like, always-attractive but sign-independent in a different way) mechanism was expected to flip sign between the opposite-sign and same-sign cases; it didn't.

ROOT CAUSE: much more consistent with each object's own internal (front-back-asymmetric poloidal) energy distribution relaxing under step_cross+sponge than with a real force between the two objects. The different t=0 dz starting values between the two runs are themselves informative: the combined phi field overlaps near the midplane (constructively for same-sign, destructively for opposite-sign), shifting the energy-weighted centroid before any stepping happens at all — the measurement conflates field-superposition geometry, internal relaxation, and (if present) real translation, without separating them.

LESSON: this measurement does NOT support "the step_cross-generated near/far u-asymmetry is a real attraction mechanism" as tested. Don't reuse this pair-centroid diagnostic to claim attraction/repulsion without first isolating internal relaxation: track centroid drift for an ISOLATED single object (no partner, same gate/settings) as a baseline first — only drift IN EXCESS of that isolated-object baseline in a pair run is attributable to the partner's presence. The "partial gravity signal, combined with lam gradients" synthesis remains unconfirmed and this diagnostic can't distinguish it from an artifact.
