CONTEXT: Tested whether the Poynting-ring probes' E/B convention
(E:=-dphi/dt, B:=curl(phi)) satisfies the local Poynting theorem
(energy conservation: dH/dt = -div(S), S=ExB) directly, on a genuinely
curl-full field.

WHAT FAILED: correlation between measured dH/dt and computed -div(S)
was -0.01 (i.e. no relationship, wrong sign if anything), with ~89%
magnitude mismatch.

ROOT CAUSE: not fully diagnosed at the time, but the leading suspect
identified afterward: S was computed via a naive 3-axis finite
difference for the divergence, on a scalar/vector layout that doesn't
match DST's actual cell/face staggering. DST's own validated conserved
quantity is H_i = 0.5*u_i^2 + 0.5*sum(phi_ik^2), which lives with
energy at cells and flux naturally living at faces (Yee-grid-like).
A naive continuum-calculus finite-difference divergence doesn't
respect that structure, so the check likely tested the wrong quantity
rather than genuinely falsifying the update rule's conservation law.

LESSON: don't retry a naive finite-difference divergence to check the
local Poynting theorem on DST fields. Any future flux/energy-
conservation check must be derived symbolically from the update rule
directly (the same method used successfully for the E_full≡0 identity
and the traveling-wave companion phi derivation), with energy-at-cell
/ flux-at-face bookkeeping matching the actual lattice, not assumed
from continuum vector calculus.
