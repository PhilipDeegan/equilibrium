CONTEXT: two related probes from exp/2026/08/27/ testing energy partition and knot drift: (1) a kick/coast energy-partition test (battery-shell kick, does energy reach and radiate from a knot's core), and (2) whether a knot drifts toward a lam-gradient "star" (probing a possible gravity-like mechanism).

WHAT FAILED: (1) exp/2026/08/27/18_24_kick_coast_energy_partition/ — after fixing a rest-phase step_cross contamination bug (same class as the A2 confound), the result showed the battery-shell kick mechanism barely reaches the knot at all: almost all "kick" energy lands directly in the injection zone near the sponge, far from the knot's core, not genuinely radiated from the object. Not the clean three-way (translation/heat/radiation) split it was designed to measure.
(2) exp/2026/08/27/18_28_knot_in_star_gradient/ — a first (R=60) attempt showed the knot's own energy centroid plateau with no drift toward the lam-gradient star. A resolution-reduced (R=35) rerun for a full star-vs-baseline comparison was dominated by centroid noise (core-mask, 137 cells, oscillating by ~1.0) — the snapshot-based "net drift" number isn't trustworthy either way.

ROOT CAUSE: (1) the injection geometry (battery shell near the sponge boundary) doesn't actually couple efficiently to the knot's core — most of the injected energy is absorbed locally rather than propagating inward. (2) a before/after snapshot centroid measurement can't distinguish real drift from ordinary centroid noise/oscillation at the tested resolution and cell-count (137 cells is too few for a stable centroid estimate over a single-snapshot comparison).

LESSON: neither result is a real "no" — both are undiagnosed measurement failures, not resolved physics. Before reusing either probe: for (1), rethink the kick injection geometry so energy actually reaches the knot's core before measuring its partition; for (2), replace the snapshot-based centroid with a time-windowed average before trusting any star-drift number, and consider more than 137 core-mask cells to reduce centroid noise.
