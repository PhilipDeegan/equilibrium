CONTEXT: validating the foundational Gauss's-law/Coulomb mechanism (probe_derived_coulomb.py) — a single fixed-u source cell relaxed under bare step() to steady state, checking Q_enc(r) conservation and u(r) shape against classical 1/r, under the sponge BC used almost everywhere else in this project to approximate free space.

WHAT FAILED: Q_enc(r) (global conservation) held robustly (radius-independent to ~1-3% at both R=40 and R=60). But the u(r) *shape* was systematically steeper than 1/r, and this deviation did NOT shrink when the domain doubled — checked at matched radius r~34: R=40 gives u*r=0.225, R=60 gives u*r=0.171 (worse, not better).

ROOT CAUSE: this refutes "the sponge is just too close to the source" as the explanation (a too-close-BC artifact should shrink with a bigger domain; this one got worse). The sponge's soft "damp toward zero" mechanism appears to genuinely distort static (Poisson-type) solutions' shape, not just localized near the boundary — Poisson solutions are globally BC-sensitive by nature, and a soft damping BC isn't the same well-posed problem as a hard grounded boundary. This was confirmed by contrast: probe_grounded_sphere.py, using the exact classical BC (u=0 fixed at the outer shell), gave a clean match (R^2=0.973->0.992) to the true 1/r-1/R solution once damping was restored (see companion failure file on the damp_max=0 attempt).

LESSON: any prior or future test in this project that used the sponge BC to approximate free space (Eddington deflection, A2, the electron/positron pair scripts) should have its radial-profile-SHAPE claims treated with real suspicion — global/conserved quantities (like Q_enc) still hold under sponge, but shape-sensitive (Poisson-type static) results do not, and this doesn't improve with domain size. Prefer an exact BC (grounded-shell style, u=0 fixed) whenever a static radial shape is the actual claim being tested.
