CONTEXT: the discrete-floor-scale search session used u_at() (Earth+Moon+rotation only) throughout to compute local throughput/tick-rate at various real-world altitudes (LEO, Galileo GSAT orbits, GPS, Moon, Tokyo Skytree), comparing against real measured clock/beat-frequency data.

WHAT FAILED: two real, non-negligible physics gaps in u_at() were found this session. (1) the Sun's own u contribution at Earth's orbital distance (~9.87e-9) is 14x BIGGER than Earth's own surface term (~6.96e-10) — never included anywhere in u_at() or any prior session's use of it. (2) for the Tokyo Skytree comparison specifically (Takamoto et al. 2020, two Sr optical lattice clocks, 450m height difference), atmospheric mass between the ground and 450m clock (shell-theorem + barometric density estimate) contributes ~6.5e-4 relative correction — about 7x BIGGER than the paper's own stated 9.1e-5 measurement precision.

ROOT CAUSE: u_at() was built as Earth+Moon+rotation only, a minimal classical model, and was never extended to include the Sun's contribution or local atmospheric/terrain mass — both of which turn out to dominate over the effects u_at() was being used to resolve at these precisions. Real high-precision clock experiments correct for this via direct on-site gravimetric surveys (measuring g directly, which automatically captures atmosphere/local terrain/buildings); u_at() captures none of it.

LESSON: every "real anchor" tick-differential number computed with u_at() this session (and probably in earlier sessions using the same function) should be read as order-of-magnitude only, not precision-matched to the source paper's own claimed precision — the missing Sun term alone dwarfs Earth's own surface signal, and the missing atmospheric term dwarfs the Skytree paper's stated precision. Before trusting any u_at()-based comparison at sub-1e-4 relative precision, add the Sun term and, for near-surface comparisons, a local atmospheric/terrain correction.
