CONTEXT: while measuring how much energy a knot-like structure radiates away vs. retains under free step() dynamics (open-vs-closed-knot probe, 2026-09-02), bc_flux()/rad_cum was used as a "how much energy left the system" diagnostic.

WHAT FAILED: bc_flux()/rad_cum sat at floating-point noise (~1e-19 to 1e-20) in BOTH the poloidal-only (proton candidate) and poloidal+toroidal (neutron candidate) runs, despite large, real, independently-confirmed energy loss in the same runs (H/H0 dropped to 0.187 and 0.594 respectively over 8000 steps — 81% and 41% of total domain energy actually gone).

ROOT CAUSE: checked the source (equilibrium/substrate/lattice.py::bc_flux, DampedLattice). It returns sum(sponge * (u_bg - u)) — a net pull of the scalar field u toward its background value in the sponge, LINEAR and SIGNED, not a quadratic energy quantity. For any dipole/multipole-symmetric field (balanced positive/negative u lobes, as in essentially all knot-like ICs in this project), that signed sum trivially cancels toward zero regardless of how much real |u|²/|phi|² energy is actually being absorbed in the same sponge region.

LESSON: bc_flux/rad_cum is not a usable "how much energy radiated" diagnostic for any non-monopolar (dipole/multipole-balanced) field. Use H(t) (total domain energy) and H_core(t) (energy within a fixed core mask) instead — these are quadratic and correctly tracked the real energy loss in this same probe. Before trusting any past or future "zero radiation" or "confinement" conclusion built on bc_flux/rad_cum, check whether the field involved has any net monopole/imbalance; if it's dipole-symmetric, treat the bc_flux reading as meaningless rather than confirmatory. This retroactively re-contextualizes the DROPPED lam-halo confinement probe's identical "bc_flux stuck at noise floor" observation (see lam-blob-knot-ic-nonsensical.txt) — that was very likely this same diagnostic mismatch, not genuine evidence of zero radiation (moot for that probe specifically since its IC was independently nonsensical, but relevant for any future radiation/confinement measurement).
