CONTEXT: Testing whether off-lattice-axis photon propagation (needing a genuine zigzag between hex/sq hops) shows more real circulation than on-axis propagation, as a candidate mechanism for curl generation (companion-phi traveling-wave construction, exp/2026/09/05/17_44_directional_current_ampere/probe_photon_staircase_circulation.py, WAVELENGTH=16, sq-axis / hex-axis / generic 24°/35° directions tested).

WHAT FAILED: By the 12-triangle plaquette circ_rms/phi_rms measure, all three directions gave the SAME large value (~0.96-0.97), converging tighter (not looser) at higher resolution — no direction dependence. Cross-checked against the OTHER, classically-validated curl measure (equilibrium.substrate.curl.b_vec_vectorized, the 4-cell square loop) before reporting this as a finding, and it disagreed sharply: ~0.0000-0.0001 for all three directions — exactly curl-free by the measure that's actually been validated against real physics.

ROOT CAUSE: The large 12-triangle number is the same "continuum-sampling curvature artifact" project_dst_discrete_curl already warned about for the dipole IC (~0.21 there) — companion-phi is built by sampling a smooth sin/cos formula, which is never an exact scalar's discrete derivative, and a shorter wavelength just makes that known artifact bigger, not a sign of real rotational content.

LESSON: The 12-triangle plaquette circulation measure is NOT reliable evidence of real curl when applied to an analytically-sampled (sin/cos) field — always cross-check against the validated 4-cell square-loop measure (equilibrium.substrate.curl.b_vec_vectorized) before trusting a nonzero plaquette result on any smooth-formula IC. The photon, like every other construction tried, is exactly curl-free by the validated measure regardless of propagation direction, and stays that way under step() (consistent with b-field-frozen-under-plain-step.txt).
